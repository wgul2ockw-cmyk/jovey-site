What was trained

Two candidates were fitted: a regularized regression on three log RGB pulse-amplitude ratios, and a compact residual temporal convolutional network with an RGB waveform encoder and an SpO₂ head. The constant baseline predicts the median training label. The ratio model had the lowest overall error and is used by the website; the CNN is retained as a research candidate.

Ratio regression improves pooled MAE by only 0.37 points (5.5%) over the constant baseline. This is a modest improvement. No confidence or clinical-validation claim follows from it.

Training and evaluation protocol

  1. Use the public per-frame R, G and B means at nominal 30 Hz. Keep both hands from a participant in the same fold.
  2. Discard the first six seconds of every recording, then create six-second windows without overlap. Exclude windows with missing labels, any reference outside 70–100%, invalid RGB, unusable mean exposure, or essentially absent channel variation. No patient-specific calibration or label-guided timing adjustment is used.
  3. Use the mean of six corresponding 1 Hz readings from the dataset’s Masimo SpO2 5 reference. Alignment follows the repository’s simultaneous-start assumption; timing and averaging lag remain limitations.
  4. Remove each RGB channel’s DC level and linear trend. Preserve relative pulse amplitudes: 100 × (RGB / channel mean − 1 − trend). Derive log AC/DC RMS ratios R/G, R/B and G/B. These visible-light camera channels are not a calibrated red/infrared pulse oximeter.
  5. For each outer test participant, select CNN epochs using a different validation participant and the remaining four for fitting. Refit on all five non-test participants using that epoch count. Fit ratio scaling and regression only on those five. No outer test participant enters training, scaling or epoch selection.
  6. Refit deployment weights on all six participants. The CNN uses 34 epochs selected from the median inner-fold epoch count. Deployed ratio regression uses fixed ridge penalty 10. The final all-data fit has no separate test cohort.

What happens on the phone

The camera’s whole-frame RGB means are sampled separately from the existing BP region. Six-second contiguous segments are resampled using measured timestamps. SpO₂ uses all three channels and its own flash, clipping, timing, pulse-variation and training-ratio checks. Three accepted segments produce an experimental average. Rejected segments and gaps are not joined.

Inference runs in the browser. Scans stay in the current tab until exported; this change adds no upload endpoint. A clear waveform, a stable output or a narrow segment range does not establish accuracy. The displayed waveform and later-entered cuff values do not enter SpO₂ inference. BP and SpO₂ retain independent models and validation.

Software verification

Python and browser JavaScript agreed on all 6 exported parity cases. Maximum difference was 0.000000586 points for regression and 0.000001462 for the CNN. These cases test implementation equivalence and were part of the final fit; they are not held-out accuracy evidence.

Checks passed for missing models, flash off, invalid timing, clipped/flat/nonfinite RGB, frame gaps, out-of-domain ratios, real RGB replay, segment separation, contact resets and expiry. Existing simulated camera/BP flows also passed. Physical iPhone capture and browser rendering were not tested in this run.

Known limits

  • Six participants, one Nexus 6P phone model; no independent iPhone or browser cohort.
  • Pulse oximeter reference rather than arterial co-oximetry.
  • Alignment uses nominal 30 Hz and shared recording start; sensor averaging/lag remain limitations.
  • Raw frame means from whole-frame decoded video; browser exposure, white balance, processing and flash differ.
  • No clinical threshold, hypoxemia triage, skin-tone subgroup, personalization or glucose validation.
  • Adjacent nonoverlapping windows remain correlated; participant count is the effective generalization sample size.
  • Live signal gates are provisional engineering rules, not clinical validation.

The present run does not implement a jointly trained BP/SpO₂/glucose encoder. The neural candidate is an initial RGB encoder plus SpO₂ head; the better-performing ratio model is the deployed baseline. Personalization remains future work requiring paired, later-session reference data.
