What changed after the retry

The first neural model had MAE 7.39 points. A second attempt tested 18 spectral regression, support-vector and tree candidates with nested participant-separated selection; its overall MAE was 7.13. The final revision uses a two-network ensemble, masks static RGB brightness-ratio features, clips standardized inputs at six standard deviations, adds dropout and stronger weight decay, balances training by participant and oxygen range, and constrains the output to 70–100% through a sigmoid head.

The revised neural MAE is 6.37 points, approximately tied with ratio regression at 6.38. The difference of 0.004 points is negligible; this is not evidence of superiority. Compared with ratio regression, low-range bias improves, but overall RMSE and the low-range 90th-percentile error worsen. The website deploys the revised neural candidate for continued research.

Training protocol

  1. Use the official Hoffman RGB means, recorded at nominal 30 Hz on Nexus 6P phones. Both hands from each person stay in that person’s fold. After a fixed initial six-second discard, create 180-frame windows without overlap.
  2. Reference labels are the mean of six 1 Hz SpO2 5 Masimo observations. Exclude missing values and windows containing reference values outside 70–100%. Follow the source repository’s common-start alignment assumption; no label-guided shift is fitted.
  3. Normalize each channel by its own DC mean and remove a linear trend while retaining AC/DC amplitudes. Input the three RGB waveforms and six log pulse-amplitude/ratio features. Static DC colour ratios are masked.
  4. For each held-out test person, use four other people for fitting and a fifth person for epoch selection. Fit all normalization statistics on training data. Refit on the five non-test people at the selected epoch count and predict the excluded test person. Repeat for two fixed seeds and average outputs.
  5. Use training-only participant × oxygen-band sample weights, Huber loss, AdamW, dropout 0.15, weight decay 0.01 and maximum 100 epochs. Inner stopping uses equal oxygen-band MAE. Final deployment networks are refitted on all six people for 20 epochs.

The same cohort was examined in earlier attempts. Participant separation prevents direct fitting on each outer test person, but repeated design choices and final candidate selection make this exploratory development. There is no untouched external cohort or final-model clinical validation. The final all-data fit is not separately tested.

Website behavior

The phone captures whole-frame RGB for SpO₂ alongside the separate BP region. After settling, each contiguous six-second segment must pass checks for browser-reported flash, measured frame timing, at least 24 fps, unclipped colour channels, repeatable variation and training-ratio range. Three accepted segments produce an experimental mean. Contact loss discards incomplete segments; retained segments expire after two minutes. No missing signal is fabricated.

Inference happens locally in the browser. Scans remain in the tab until exported. Results and exports identify the model, raw accepted RGB, source-test error and limitations. The normalized display waveform is not used for inference. A stable result or narrow segment spread is not a confidence interval.

The BP model and cuff-reference flow remain separate. This run does not train glucose, add personalization, integrate EMR into SpO₂ inference, or train a joint BP/SpO₂/glucose encoder.

Verification and limits

Python and JavaScript neural predictions agree on six parity cases to within 0.00000664 percentage points. Parity cases are final-fit data and verify implementation only. Real RGB replay, participant separation, missing models, invalid timing, flash off, clipping, gaps, flat signals, domain rejection, contact reset, expiry, model loading and scan/report exports were checked in software. Physical iPhone performance and browser rendering were not tested.

  • Only six participants and one source phone model; subgroup accuracy is unknown.
  • Reference labels come from a pulse oximeter, not arterial co-oximetry.
  • Phone/browser exposure, white balance, tone mapping, flash and frame resizing differ from the source study.
  • Adjacent windows and the two hands remain correlated even without overlap; 1,908 windows are not 1,908 independent people.
  • Live quality gates and three-segment averaging have no separate clinical evaluation.
